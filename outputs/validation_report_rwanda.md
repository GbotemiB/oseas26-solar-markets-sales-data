# Validation report: Rwanda off-grid solar access map

Generated by `python -m src.report` from the result files in `outputs/`. Household-weighted mean absolute error (MAE) of the grid, solar and unserved shares, averaged over the three. Lower is better.

## 1. Random vs spatial cross-validation (5 folds)

Random folds mix clusters from the same district across train and test. Spatial folds hold out whole districts (`GroupKFold`, groups = district), so a test cluster is predicted by a model that never saw its district. The spatial score is the honest one. Core feature set (7 features).

| model | random_cv | spatial_cv | spatial_fold_min | spatial_fold_max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| logistic regression | 0.152 | 0.154 | 0.130 | 0.170 |
| random forest | 0.154 | 0.158 | 0.134 | 0.174 |
| lightgbm (regularised) | 0.155 | 0.159 | 0.137 | 0.174 |
| extra trees | 0.156 | 0.159 | 0.137 | 0.174 |
| catboost | 0.153 | 0.160 | 0.135 | 0.176 |
| xgboost | 0.156 | 0.162 | 0.137 | 0.178 |
| elastic-net logistic | 0.161 | 0.162 | 0.129 | 0.190 |
| lightgbm | 0.158 | 0.167 | 0.143 | 0.184 |
| constant (national mean) | 0.208 | 0.209 | 0.194 | 0.222 |

- The shipped model is logistic regression: spatial error 0.154, 26% lower than the constant national-mean map (0.209).
- **Checkpoint not met:** LightGBM (0.167) does not beat the logistic baseline (0.154) in spatial CV, so scope was cut to the logistic model. With about 560 clusters the simplest model is as good as the complex ones.
- Random and spatial error are almost the same (gap +0.002). Neighbouring clusters leaking information is a small problem at district level here. The spatial score is still the one to quote.

## 2. Uncertainty ranges

Split conformal prediction on spatial out-of-fold errors. Target coverage 80%. Coverage is checked with nested spatial folds: calibrate on four folds of districts, test on the fifth.

| share | target | coverage | mean_range_width |
|---|---|---|---|
| grid | 80% | 81.1% | 0.60 |
| solar | 80% | 79.6% | 0.37 |
| unserved | 80% | 80.4% | 0.41 |

- Coverage holds on average. It does not hold for every cell or every district.
- The ranges are wide because they are calibrated on cluster-level errors, which include household sampling noise (about 25 households per cluster) and GPS displacement. They overstate the uncertainty of the true cell share.
- Do-not-trust mask (22,435 predicted cells): 27% flagged for a wide range (a ranking: the widest 25%), 19% flagged because their features lie outside the range seen at survey clusters, 44% flagged by either. A fixed 0.4 width limit would flag 94% of cells, so the width flag is relative.

## 3. Control totals against independent published numbers

Predicted cell shares are summed (population-weighted) per area and compared with published survey and census numbers. These sources were not used to train the model. "Served" is grid + solar. Sources are from different years: Census August 2022, EICV7 2023/24, DHS June to October 2025.

| level | source | quantity | n | mae | bias | pearson | spearman | skill |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| district | EICV7 2023/24 | served (grid+solar) | 30 | 0.054 | 0.018 | 0.748 | 0.388 | 0.306 |
| sector | Census 2022 | served (grid+solar) | 415 | 0.133 | 0.127 | 0.752 | 0.509 | 0.299 |
| sector | Census 2022 (C1.5) | grid connected | 415 | 0.085 | 0.019 | 0.818 | 0.654 | 0.535 |
| sector | Census 2022 (C1.3 minus C1.5) | solar proxy (rough) | 415 | 0.111 | 0.108 | 0.618 | 0.664 | 0.048 |
| province | EICV7 2023/24 | grid | 5 | 0.054 | 0.045 | 0.974 | 0.700 | 0.612 |
| province | EICV7 2023/24 | solar | 5 | 0.032 | -0.030 | 0.986 | 0.900 | 0.576 |
| province | Census 2022 | grid | 5 | 0.072 | 0.072 | 0.986 | 0.700 | 0.547 |
| province | Census 2022 | solar | 5 | 0.043 | 0.043 | 0.970 | 0.900 | 0.108 |

Skill = 1 minus MAE divided by the MAE of a flat map at the national mean. Bias = predicted minus published.

- Districts: MAE 0.054, skill 0.31, but rank order is weak (Spearman 0.39). The map gets district levels roughly right and their order only partly.
- Sectors vs Census 2022: bias +0.127. Most of this is likely the 2022 vs 2025 timing gap (access grew between the two), not model error alone. After removing the bias, MAE is 0.082.
- Grid share at 415 sectors vs Census Table C1.5 (households connected to the grid): Pearson 0.82, Spearman 0.65, MAE 0.085, skill 0.54. This is the strongest independent check: the grid pattern is predictable. "Connected" is not "grid is the main light", so read the pattern, not the level.
- Solar proxy at the same sectors (electrified minus connected, clipped at 0): Pearson 0.62, Spearman 0.66, skill 0.05, bias +0.108. The proxy is rough (it gives 8.5% nationally against 13.9% in Table 8.12), so it supports the ranking of sectors but says nothing reliable about the level.
- Provinces: Southern has the largest grid over-prediction vs EICV7 (+12.0 points).

| province | grid_pred_minus_eicv7 | solar_pred_minus_eicv7 |
|---|---|---|
| Eastern | -1.5 | -2.1 |
| City of Kigali | +2.0 | +0.9 |
| Western | +4.1 | -4.2 |
| Northern | +6.9 | -1.6 |
| Southern | +12.0 | -6.1 |

Below province level the solar share can only be checked through the rough proxy above. A direct grid / solar split exists in published tables only by province.

## 4. Solar vs unserved among non-grid households

The market-reality layer depends on separating solar from unserved households. Target: solar / (solar + unserved) among non-grid households, spatial CV, weighted by non-grid households.

| model | n_clusters | mae | skill | auc_solar_majority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| flat | 472 | 0.202 | 0.00 | 0.41 |
| lights only | 472 | 0.192 | 0.05 | 0.56 |
| lights + population | 472 | 0.191 | 0.05 | 0.58 |
| ratio model (7 core features) | 472 | 0.185 | 0.08 | 0.64 |
| our map (multinomial) | 472 | 0.185 | 0.08 | 0.65 |

- The model (0.185) is only 8% better than a flat map and -0.007 vs night lights alone (0.192); the 95% interval of that difference is [-0.016, +0.001] (paired bootstrap by district).
- The decision rule (clearly better than night lights) is **not met**. The market-reality layer is a low-confidence hypothesis.

## 5. Comparison with the naive "dense and dark" index

Naive index = population x darkness (from night lights), the method the challenge brief warns about. Compared with our layer at cell level and, on held-out districts, against the survey.

- Cells: Spearman correlation between the naive index and our layer -0.18. 13% of the naive top 10% is also in our top 10% (18% counted in unserved households; chance 10%). Our mask flags 53% of the naive top 10%; predicted solar share there is 23% against 20% nationally.
- Held-out districts (389 clusters): AUC for the top tercile of the survey unserved / (solar + unserved) is 0.48 for the naive index, 0.39 for night lights alone and 0.70 for our model. Ours minus naive: +0.22 (95% interval +0.10 to +0.34).
- Same folds as section 1. The naive index and lights are not fitted; our model is. Section 4 scores the error of the solar share, this section scores the ranking, so the two tests need not agree. Details in `naive_baseline_<country>.md`.

## 6. What was not validated

- No direct check of the solar vs unserved split below province level (only the rough sector proxy above).
- The World Bank Multi-Tier Framework (MTF) data was not used.
- GOGLA sales are national and affiliate-only; see `calibration_note_rwanda.md`. The maps are not rescaled.
- Time change was not tested, and no second country was run.
- One of 416 census sectors did not match a boundary name.
