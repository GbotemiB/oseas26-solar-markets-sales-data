# Validation report: Nigeria off-grid solar access map

Generated by `python -m src.report` from the result files in `outputs/`. Household-weighted mean absolute error (MAE) of the grid, solar and unserved shares, averaged over the three. Lower is better.

## 1. Random vs spatial cross-validation (5 folds)

Random folds mix clusters from the same district across train and test. Spatial folds hold out whole districts (`GroupKFold`, groups = district), so a test cluster is predicted by a model that never saw its district. The spatial score is the honest one. Core feature set (7 features).

| model | random_cv | spatial_cv | spatial_fold_min | spatial_fold_max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| lightgbm | 0.143 | 0.144 | 0.137 | 0.154 |
| catboost | 0.148 | 0.147 | 0.138 | 0.152 |
| xgboost | 0.147 | 0.147 | 0.137 | 0.153 |
| lightgbm (regularised) | 0.149 | 0.150 | 0.141 | 0.155 |
| random forest | 0.149 | 0.150 | 0.140 | 0.159 |
| logistic regression | 0.162 | 0.163 | 0.153 | 0.168 |
| elastic-net logistic | 0.165 | 0.164 | 0.161 | 0.172 |
| extra trees | 0.168 | 0.168 | 0.160 | 0.175 |
| constant (national mean) | 0.272 | 0.272 | 0.266 | 0.280 |

- The shipped model is logistic regression: spatial error 0.163, 40% lower than the constant national-mean map (0.272).
- Checkpoint met: LightGBM (0.144) beats the logistic baseline in spatial CV.
- Random and spatial error are almost the same (gap +0.001). Neighbouring clusters leaking information is a small problem at district level here. The spatial score is still the one to quote.

- Label note: Households with electricity whose main light is a torch (power cuts) are counted as grid, so the grid share mixes grid access with grid supply (flag share_grid_unreliable).

## 2. Uncertainty ranges

Split conformal prediction on spatial out-of-fold errors. Target coverage 80%. Coverage is checked with nested spatial folds: calibrate on four folds of districts, test on the fifth.

| share | target | coverage | mean_range_width |
|---|---|---|---|
| grid | 80% | 80.0% | 0.51 |
| solar | 80% | 80.2% | 0.14 |
| unserved | 80% | 80.1% | 0.48 |

- Coverage holds on average. It does not hold for every cell or every district.
- The ranges are wide because they are calibrated on cluster-level errors, which include household sampling noise (about 25 households per cluster) and GPS displacement. They overstate the uncertainty of the true cell share.
- Do-not-trust mask (910,803 predicted cells): 25% flagged for a wide range (a ranking: the widest 25%), 32% flagged because their features lie outside the range seen at survey clusters, 53% flagged by either. A fixed 0.4 width limit would flag 60% of cells, so the width flag is relative.

## 3. Control totals against independent published numbers

No external control totals are available for Nigeria: the open published tables used for Rwanda (EICV7, Census 2022) have no counterpart here, so the map is checked only against the survey it was trained on (sections 1 and 2) and against the naive baseline. Treat the levels with extra caution.

## 4. Solar vs unserved among non-grid households

The market-reality layer depends on separating solar from unserved households. Target: solar / (solar + unserved) among non-grid households, spatial CV, weighted by non-grid households.

| model | n_clusters | mae | skill | auc_solar_majority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| flat | 1136 | 0.177 | 0.00 | 0.46 |
| lights only | 1136 | 0.161 | 0.09 | 0.78 |
| lights + population | 1136 | 0.148 | 0.16 | 0.84 |
| ratio model (7 core features) | 1136 | 0.144 | 0.18 | 0.86 |
| our map (multinomial) | 1136 | 0.144 | 0.19 | 0.86 |

- The model (0.144) is 18% better than a flat map and -0.017 vs night lights alone (0.161); the 95% interval of that difference is [-0.023, -0.011] (paired bootstrap by district).
- The decision rule (clearly better than night lights) is **met**. The market-reality layer has better support here than a pure guess, but it rests on one survey: still a hypothesis.

## 5. Comparison with the naive "dense and dark" index

Naive index = population x darkness (from night lights), the method the challenge brief warns about. Compared with our layer at cell level and, on held-out districts, against the survey.

- Cells: Spearman correlation between the naive index and our layer -0.50. 0% of the naive top 10% is also in our top 10% (0% counted in unserved households; chance 10%). Our mask flags 68% of the naive top 10%; predicted solar share there is 9% against 7% nationally.
- Held-out districts (861 clusters): AUC for the top tercile of the survey unserved / (solar + unserved) is 0.48 for the naive index, 0.65 for night lights alone and 0.66 for our model. Ours minus naive: +0.17 (95% interval +0.10 to +0.25).
- Same folds as section 1. The naive index and lights are not fitted; our model is. Section 4 scores the error of the solar share, this section scores the ranking, so the two tests need not agree. Details in `naive_baseline_nigeria.md`.

## 6. What was not validated

- No independent check of the solar vs unserved split at any level: only the survey it was trained on.
- No GOGLA calibration and no census or survey control totals for this country.
