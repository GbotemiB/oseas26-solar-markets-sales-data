# Cross-country comparison

Independent runs of the same pipeline, each trained and tested on its own country's DHS survey. This is a check that the method replicates, not a transfer of one model to another country. Generated by `python -m src.cross_country` from the aggregate result files in `outputs/`.

## Headline

- Spatial CV error is below the flat national-mean map in 3 of 3 countries (rwanda, kenya, nigeria).
- Our model ranks the survey unserved / (solar + unserved) better than the naive "dense and dark" index, with a 95% interval above zero, in 3 of 3 countries (rwanda, kenya, nigeria).
- Solar vs unserved is clearly better than night lights alone (decision rule met) in: kenya, nigeria; not met in: rwanda.
- National shares from the map differ from the survey by more than 5 points in: kenya, nigeria. Quote levels for those countries with caution.

Spatial-CV groups are the ADM2 units of each country (Rwanda districts, Kenya constituencies, Nigeria local government areas), so groups are smaller where the survey is sparser and the spatial score is then less strict. The solar test column is MAE of the ratio model minus MAE of night lights alone (negative = better); the rule is met when its 95% paired-bootstrap interval is below zero. AUC is for ranking clusters in the top third of unserved / (solar + unserved).

## National shares: map against survey

| country | grid map % | grid survey % | grid bias (pts) | solar map % | solar survey % | solar bias (pts) | unserved map % | unserved survey % | unserved bias (pts) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| rwanda | 53.4 | 53.9 | -0.5 | 19.7 | 19.6 | +0.1 | 26.9 | 26.5 | +0.4 |
| kenya | 48.5 | 56.5 | -8.0 | 28.6 | 27.9 | +0.8 | 22.9 | 15.7 | +7.3 |
| nigeria | 46.0 | 51.7 | -5.7 | 6.6 | 6.8 | -0.1 | 47.3 | 41.5 | +5.8 |

Map shares are population-weighted over predicted cells. Survey shares are household-weighted DHS. A large gap means the 1 km map does not reproduce the survey level even where the cluster-level model is unbiased. In Kenya and Nigeria this was traced to aggregation, not to the model: the map weights cells by people while the survey weights households (urban households are smaller), and WorldPop places fewer people in urban cells than the survey sample does. Weighting by households would close about half of Kenya's gap and most of Nigeria's. This was diagnosed but not applied.
